Retrieved 2026-10-08. Every value below was pulled from a page, PDF, label image or database record. Anything I couldn't retrieve says **not found**. Full detail, with per-row URLs, is in `comparison.csv`.

**For the pediatrician:** calories and protein are the headline differences. Cells that differ meaningfully from PediaSure are marked ⚠️.

## Three things to know first

1. **Your benchmark doesn't match PediaSure's current label on two values.** Abbott's current published label (product data dated 2026-09-05, matching the Target page) shows **5 g added sugar and <1 g fiber** for the vanilla bottle. Your benchmark has 12 g added sugar and 3 g fiber. Calories (240), protein (7 g), calcium (330 mg), vitamin D (6 mcg), potassium (470 mg), sodium (90 mg) and iron (2.7 mg) all match. I could not confirm **32 mg DHA** on any current label panel. PediaSure lists tuna oil as an ingredient but no DHA amount. Check your bottle. The table uses the current Abbott/Target label as the reference.
2. **I could not read Walmart's pages.** Walmart serves a "Robot or human?" wall, so Parent's Choice nutrition came from **USDA FoodData Central records dated 2022–2023**. Those may not match the bottle on shelves now. Walmart prices come from search-result snippets only (not verified on the page).
3. **No Kirkland (Costco) or Amazon Basics pediatric shake turned up.** Kirkland results were infant formula only. Target's up&up is included instead.

## Verdict

**Nutritionally closest (reasonable swap candidates to raise with the pediatrician):**
- **up&up (Target).** Same 240 kcal and 7 g protein, with essentially the same ingredient list as PediaSure. The current chocolate label matches on the headline minerals (calcium 330, iron 2.7, vitamin D 6, potassium 470). The up&up vanilla record is older and shows 12 g added sugar. It contains milk and soy like PediaSure.
- **Parent's Choice (Walmart), cheapest at about $1.49–$1.58.** Calories and protein match (240 kcal, 7–8 g). The records I found show lower calcium (250 mg), vitamin D (about 4 mcg) and potassium (310 mg) for vanilla, plus 13–18 g of sugar. Those are 2022–2023 records, so check the current bottle before relying on them. The fiber version adds about 3 g of fiber.
- **Kate Farms Kids**, only if dairy- and soy-free matters. Calories (about 237 per 8 fl oz) and protein (about 7.6 g) are close, with iron and vitamin D as high as or higher than PediaSure. Calcium is lower (280 mg), fiber is 5 g, and it is plant-based, so it is a different formula rather than a like-for-like swap. It is also the most expensive.

**Not close enough:**
- **Boost Kid Essentials 1.5.** 360 kcal and 10 g protein is 50% more calories and 43% more protein per serving. It is a calorie-dense formula, not a like-for-like swap.
- **Orgain Kids Protein.** 180 kcal is 25% fewer calories, with almost no iron (0.1 mg vs 2.7 mg) and low potassium (110 mg). It is labeled for ages 4 and up.
- **PediaSure powder (prepared).** The same brand, but 220 kcal and 6 g protein (−8% and −14%). Calcium (180 mg), vitamin D (3.6 mcg) and potassium (280 mg) are all well below the bottle. It is labeled supplemental only, not sole-source.

**Flag for the pediatrician:** if your daughter relies on this for calories or protein, the 20 kcal / 1 g gap in the powder and the large gaps in Boost and Orgain are the ones to ask about. If she is on a restricted diet, ask about the DHA amount, which I could only confirm for Kate Farms (35 mg).
